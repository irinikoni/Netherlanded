The laws involved

Two separate questions: may the app read/write on the phone, and may any data leave the phone?

Dutch cookie rule

Telecommunicatiewet 11.7a

Storing or reading data on a phone needs consent unless it is strictly necessary or analytics with little privacy impact by the service provider itself. Netherlanded is that provider, so Tier 1 may run without consent while it measures the app's own quality.

EU privacy law

GDPR / AVG

No student accounts exist. Tier 1: legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) with the right to object shown clearly (Art. 21(4)). Tier 2: specific opt-in consent (Art. 6(1)(a), 7) naming both purposes, withdrawable anytime.

Dutch implementation

UAVG, age 16

16 is the Dutch age for consenting to online services, so Terms v2.2 require age 16+. Students aged 16–17 remain legal minors, carrying heavy weight in every legitimate-interest test.

Consumer & cyber law

Civil Code & NIS2 (Cbw)

Terms use consumer law (statutory competent court, mandatory rights preserved). Storing no student identifiers on servers keeps vendor risk low for universities under the Dutch Cyberbeveiligingswet.

Who is responsible

Roles are set per processing operation, not per company. Netherlanded will be run by an eenmanszaak: the owner is personally the controller and liable.

What universities can see: the 7-step disclosure pipeline

Staff see only read-only snapshots of their own institution via nl.release_json_for_institution(), checked again by nl.assert_release_safe(). Dashboard access is written into the contract.
